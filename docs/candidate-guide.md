# Candidate guide

What to expect in your live coding interview, and how to use the workspace. Nothing to install: you work in your browser.

Your interviewer will send you a link. Open it in an up to date browser on a laptop or desktop. Use the whole link exactly as you received it: the long code at the end is what lets you in. It works for this interview only.

## 1. Open the link and enter your name

Type your name and click **Start Challenge**. Use the same name if you come back later: your work is saved and comes back with it. If the name is already taken, add your last name or a number.

## 2. A short tour

The first time you open the tool, a short tour shows up. Read it in a minute, then click **Done**. You can open it again any time from the menu at the top right, under **Help**.

## 3. Your workspace

- **Left:** the files of the exercise.
- **Middle:** the editor. Files with a lock are read only, like the exercise brief.
- **Bottom:** a terminal, with a **Run** button that starts the exercise.
- **Right:** your AI assistant.

Your interviewer sees your work live and shares the next phase when you are ready. Your work saves by itself a few seconds after you stop typing.

## 4. Choose your assistant

The first time, the panel on the right asks how you want to work with the assistant. You can pick from:

- **Agent mode**, a chat panel, with a choice of model and a plan mode.
- **Claude Code**, the terminal program.
- **OpenCode**, another terminal program.

Pick the one you know best. You can change your mind with **Change** at the top of the panel; the one you were using stops, and you can pick it again later. Your interviewer decides which of these are offered, so you may see fewer.

## 5. Read the brief

Start with `description.md`. It explains the exercise and how it will go. More phases appear during the interview, when your interviewer shares them.

## 6. Work with the assistant

Use it the way you would at work: ask it to explain code, find bugs, write and review changes. It is part of the interview.

- Say what you are trying to do and why. Read what it writes before you accept it.
- It edits your files directly. In the chat, the changes show in the editor and you can keep or undo each one.
- Think aloud. Your interviewer is interested in how you work, not only in the result.

## 7. Run your code

Click **Run** (or press Cmd or Ctrl and Enter). If the exercise has tests, **Run tests** runs them and the chip next to it shows how many pass. Click the chip to see each test, and the arrow next to a test runs only that one. **Run both** runs your code and then the tests. A web app opens in a preview tab. Cmd or Ctrl and K opens a palette of everything you can do.

## Try it first

You can look around a replica of this workspace before your interview, with nothing to install and nothing sent anywhere: [open the live demo](https://guicheffer.github.io/trio-interview/demo/). It shows the Fibonacci exercise, and **Run** really runs the code in your browser. The assistant is shown but not connected, so nobody can send it a message there. A small switcher changes which assistant is displayed: the chat, Claude Code or OpenCode.

## If something breaks

Tell your interviewer. Reloading the page keeps your work.
